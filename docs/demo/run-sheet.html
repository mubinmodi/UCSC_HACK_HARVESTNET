Run sheet

If a moment runs 10 seconds past its mark, skip to the next one. Read every number from the screen; never say one that isn't shown.

0:00slides 1–3

Opening

Good produce goes to waste every day, while families nearby could use it. HarvestNet gives every donor and every food bank its own AI agent. They negotiate how each batch is shared. Nobody sits in the middle and decides.

0:15slide 6
Level 1

1. A household asks, in Spanish

Caller: calls the voice line in Spanish (family of four, near Watsonville, after 6 pm, small fridge, gets CalFresh). If voice is down, type the same answers into the household page.

The assistant reads it all back and asks before it saves anything. Their weekly fair share comes from the calculator, not from the AI.

Proves: no app, no account, no name or address. If it fails: "Voice is down, so we use the web page. Same questions, same read-back."

0:40slide 6
Level 1

2. The farmers market reports surplus

Operator: on the donor page, report apples, romaine and potatoes with a "must be gone by" time and drop-off available, then press Offer this surplus.

At closing time, a farmers market has extra produce that must be gone today.

Proves: any donor can join.

0:55slides 7–8
Level 3

3. The agents negotiate

Operator: open the lot's version history on the donor page or a food bank dashboard. If it's still running at 1:15, show the finished history instead of waiting.

The food banks bid item by item. Every change is a new version, with a reason. The advocate speaks for the households furthest below their fair share. It can propose, but it can't vote.

Proves: real disagreement, no central decider.

1:25slide 7
Level 3

4. A live approval

Approvers: open the review email ("HarvestNet: review version …") and approve on the page it links to. Or press Accept on the dashboard; it asks you to confirm the exact version.

Each manager approves the exact version. If anyone changes the terms, old approvals stop counting. Nothing is ever committed automatically.

If email is slow: use the dashboard's Accept, or the local review link. Say "This is the local approval page."

1:40slide 9
Level 2

5. The cooler fails

Operator: flip the city food bank's cooler switch with the command below, wait about 15 seconds, then open the city dashboard. The city food bank's own agent notices the failed reading, proposes a new version, and the review emails go out. Reason cards for every candidate appear under the cooler failure.

curl -sS -X POST -H "Authorization: Bearer $DEMO_SWITCH_TOKEN" -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  https://18-217-110-223.sslip.io/systems/demo/city/cooler -d '{"failed": true}'

The reply shows "status": "failed" and "synthetic": true. A 401 means the token is wrong or missing. Read the token once, before the run (it is a secret, so don't put it on screen):

export DEMO_SWITCH_TOKEN=$(aws secretsmanager get-secret-value --profile incubator --region us-east-2 \
  --secret-id harvestnet/platform --query SecretString --output text \
  | python3 -c 'import sys, json; print(json.load(sys.stdin)["DEMO_SWITCH_TOKEN"])')

To reset (after the run, and before the next one), send the same command with {"failed": false}. The reading goes back to working and the watcher records it. The switch only changes the reading; a change both managers approved stays in the plan. To run moment 5 again, start from a fresh offer (moments 2–4). A version a manager declined is not proposed again.

Now the city's walk-in cooler fails. Its agent checks the produce profiles: romaine must stay cold, apples are fine. It searches the Directory for food banks with cold space and writes a reason card for each one. It's a new version, so both managers approve again.

If the switch or Directory fails: open the replay at the cooler step and say "This is a recorded replay of the same scenario, made from our code."

6. The household gets an email

Caller: show the pickup email on the phone, or the pickup screen on the household page.

The parent gets an email in Spanish: which food bank, what time, what's in it, and their code. If they can't make it, one tap passes it to the next family.

Proves: a household counts as reached only when it confirms pickup.

7. The results strip

Presenter: switch to the results strip and read the numbers from the screen.

We compare against first-come-first-served on the same inputs, in synthetic scenarios. We show where negotiation does better and where it does worse.

Every pound is counted once: collected by households, committed to a food bank that can hand it out in time, or wasted. LLM results show "Not run" until the paid Claude runs happen; say so if asked.

When something fails

Switch as soon as a step fails, and say the label out loud. Every fallback is labeled on screen.

If this failsSwitch toSay
Voice line (ElevenLabs)Household page, same questions and read-back"Voice is down, so we use the web page."
Email (n8n or SES)Dashboard Accept, or the local review link; show the pickup screen"This is the local approval page."
Cooler switch command (401, or no change after 30 seconds)Check the token; if it still fails, open the replay at the cooler step"This is a recorded replay of the same scenario, made from our code."
Organization systems (Beeceptor)Recorded readings, labeled replay"These readings are a recorded copy."
Directory (AGNTCY)Local Directory, flagged on the dashboard"Discovery is running locally today."
Claude (cap or outage)Rules-based negotiators, automatically"These moves come from the rules-based negotiators."
Network or the whole systemOffline replay on a laptop"This is a recorded replay, made from our code. It is not live."

Never say

  • "The AI decides who gets food." Agents negotiate; people approve.
  • "We fed X people." We count households that confirmed a pickup.
  • "The LLM agents beat the baseline." They weren't run for these results.
  • Any number that isn't on a screen or a slide.
