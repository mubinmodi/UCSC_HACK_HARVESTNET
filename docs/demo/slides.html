HarvestNet

Surplus fruit and vegetables, shared fairly with low-income households, by agents that negotiate. No one in the middle decides.

Every organization keeps its own keys. The food still moves.

All data in this demo is synthetic. Results are labeled synthetic, recorded, or not run.

The problem

Farms, grocers, and farmers markets end up with good produce they can't sell. It spoils within days. Nearby, low-income families would use it.

The hard part is splitting it fairly and fast:

  • A food bank in a low-access area may get far less than one near the donors.
  • A family that works until 6 pm can't collect lettuce at 2 pm.
  • A small donor has no truck.
  • When a food bank's cooler fails, the greens in it are lost.

Who negotiates: no central decider

Donor agent

Wants the produce used, with little extra work. Would rather a food bank collects.

Food bank agents

City, rural, eastside. Want what their households can collect and use. Compete for limited produce.

Advocate agent

Speaks for the households furthest below their fair share. Proposes changes. No vote.

  • Each agent has specialists that check facts with calculators. The AI never invents a number.
  • They edit one shared, numbered draft agreement, item by item, including who moves the produce.
  • Each organization's own human approves the exact version. Nothing commits on its own.

Fair share, like a SNAP benefit

Each household has a weekly produce amount that grows with household size. It is the yardstick for fairness, not an allocation.

Each extra person+2 lb

  • SNAP/CalFresh is optional and self-reported. "Prefer not to say" is always offered.
  • The answer never changes the fair share.
  • Farmers markets can join as donors.
  • A Market Match tip goes only to SNAP households, and only for a verified nearby market.
  • No name, no address, no income. Requests expire after 7 days.

The demo in seven moments

  1. A parent calls the voice line in Spanish and hears their fair share. Level 1
  2. A farmers market reports end-of-day surplus. Level 1
  3. Food bank and donor agents negotiate item by item; the advocate argues from fair-share gaps. Level 3
  4. Managers approve the exact version. No one approves for anyone else. Level 3
  5. The city cooler fails. The Directory finds a food bank with cold space; reason cards explain why. Level 2
  6. The parent can get an email with the food bank, time, items, and code if they provided an address. Evidence

In the recorded rules-based run, the draft goes from version 1 to version 14. Each change has a reason:

City food bank: "Our 4 households are 31 lb below their fair share this week. 200 lb of romaine lettuce is unclaimed. …"
Farmers market: "Asking City food bank to collect by our 3 pm deadline, since we can't drop off there."
Rural food bank: "Asking Farmers market to drop off our 32 lb, as it offered, since we have no truck free today. …"

Every named organization accepts version 14, places holds on its own resources, and its own human approves that exact version. Then it is a commitment.

6  The household hears back Evidence

  • A pickup email names the food bank, the pickup window, the items and pounds, and the household's code.
  • Pounds come from the portion calculator, up to the fair share. Never guessed by an AI.
  • The "Can't make it" link opens the household page; tapping its release button gives the portion to the next household furthest below fair share.
  • If a cooler change moves a pickup to a food bank the household didn't choose, the email explains the change and offers that link.
  • A household counts as reached only when it confirms pickup.

Emails are in the household's language. Spanish produce names come from the reviewed produce profiles. This synthetic scenario contains no addresses and sends no email.

When to stop, keep the disagreement, or ask a human

RuleWhat happens
AgreementEvery named organization approves the same version, so it becomes a commitment.
Ask a humanAny condition doubt, or any change to a committed plan.
Keep the disagreement3 rounds with no new terms. Dashboards show the options and each side's reason.
DeadlineNo commitment by the donor's deadline: the produce is unclaimed, with reasons. Nothing commits on its own.

In the shipped cooler-failure scenario the eastside manager answers only when the planned trip is due, so the core refuses the late answer and the romaine waits for a human. That is reported as is.

Architecture and sponsors: none of them decides

Cisco AGNTCY

A2A messages in the negotiation room. Directory finds food banks by capability.

Claude

Negotiators, the advocate, and the front-desk assistant. Calculators supply every number.

ElevenLabs

The voice line, with English and Spanish read-back.

n8n

Pickup and human-review email through Amazon SES.

Beeceptor

Mock inventory, storage, cooler status, and truck schedules, with a cooler switch.

AWS

Hosting for the agents, the API, and the web pages.

Fallbacks if a sponsor is down: recorded readings for Beeceptor, the text form for voice, local approval links for n8n, in-process messages for AGNTCY, and a recorded replay.
