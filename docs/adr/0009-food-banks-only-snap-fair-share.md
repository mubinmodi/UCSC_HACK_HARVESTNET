# Food banks only, transport per deal, and a SNAP-style fair share

October 10, 2026, decided by the user: remove volunteer drivers and the coordinator, have households collect from food banks they choose, and "think of SNAP benefits." This narrows ADRs 0006–0008.

## Decision

- **Pickup sites are food banks only.** Households choose which food banks they'll collect from. Community fridges are deferred.
- **No volunteer drivers and no coordinator role.** Who moves the produce is a term in each draft agreement: the donor drops it off, or the food bank collects it with its own truck. The agents negotiate which.
- **SNAP, in three ways:**
  1. **Fair share.** Each household has a weekly produce amount that grows with household size, like a SNAP benefit. The participating food banks publish the table as a shared, versioned policy, and calculators apply it.
  2. **Eligibility signal.** Households may say they receive SNAP/CalFresh. It's optional and self-reported, with no proof. Food banks may use it under their own rules.
  3. **SNAP produce programs.** Farmers markets join as donors, and SNAP households can be told where their CalFresh dollars are matched for fresh produce, such as Market Match. Participating markets must be verified.

## Consequences

- The fair-share table is a published shared policy, not a central decider. It assigns no produce, and every donor and food bank still decides for itself (ADR 0001).
- Proposed: SNAP participation never changes a household's fair share, so disclosing it brings no advantage and no penalty.
- Level 2 now comes from a food bank's storage failure (for example, a failed walk-in cooler). Its agent rediscovers food banks with cold space through the Directory, with reason cards.
- Coverage is measured against fair share: the share of its weekly fair share each household received.
- Disagreements and approvals appear on the dashboards of the donors and food banks involved.
