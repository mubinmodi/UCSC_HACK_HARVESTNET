# Questionnaire for a food-rescue coordinator

**Status:** draft. Not sent. Only the user decides whether to send it, and to whom.

**Who it's for:** someone who arranges surplus-food transfers day to day, for example at a food bank's produce program, a farm-to-food-bank program, or a gleaning organization.

**What we need back:** whether HarvestNet's picture of the job is right, and where coordination actually breaks down. Short answers are fine.

---

Hi! We're students building a hackathon prototype about how farms, food banks, truckers, and funders agree on surplus food transfers. We'd really value 10 minutes of your experience. There are no wrong answers, and we won't quote you without permission.

1. **Walk us through your last surplus transfer that almost fell through.** What nearly broke it: transport, money, storage, timing, food condition, or something else?

2. **How many people or organizations usually have to say yes before food moves?** Who are they, and roughly how long does it take to get all the yeses?

3. **How do you find out what's available and who can take it today?** For example phone, email, a shared spreadsheet, a platform. What takes the most time?

4. **When a truck or driver falls through, what happens next?** How do you find a replacement, and how often does the food get lost anyway?

5. **When two recipients want the same food, how is that decided today?** Who has the final say, and does anyone speak for communities that don't usually get much?

6. **Who pays for picking, packing, and transport, and how is that negotiated?** Does any funder attach conditions to its money?

7. **If a tool showed you a "needs attention" list, what would you want on it first?** For example approvals waiting, failed bookings, deadlines, disagreements.

8. **Would you trust a system where each organization's AI assistant proposes terms, but every organization still approves its own commitments?** What would make you trust it, or not?

**Optional:** is there anything about this work that outsiders usually get wrong?

Thank you!
