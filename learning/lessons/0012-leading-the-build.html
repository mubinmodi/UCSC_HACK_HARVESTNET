Leading the build

The build order, a way to split the work, and what to catch in reviews.

After this lessonYou can split the build across your team and spot changes that break the design.

Build order, and why

  • 1. Records and rules-based negotiation in one process. The core has to work before any AI or sponsor is added.
  • 2. Produce profiles and the fair-share calculator. Every matching rule depends on them.
  • 3. Web pages for households, donors, and food banks.
  • 4. Deploy to AWS early, so n8n and ElevenLabs can call in.
  • 5. AGNTCY: A2A, the Directory, and the cooler-failure scenario.
  • 6. LLM negotiators and the assistant, with calculator tools and number checks.
  • 7. n8n, then the ElevenLabs voice line.
  • 8. Evaluation runs, dashboards, and demo rehearsal.

Source: docs/MVP_DESIGN.md, section 18.

A way to split the work (suggestion)

  • Person A: protocol and records. Draft versions, holds, approvals, stop rules, calculators.
  • Person B: agents. Donor, food bank, and advocate negotiators, rules-based first, then LLM.
  • Person C: households and the web. Pages, the front-desk assistant, ElevenLabs.
  • Person D: infrastructure and evidence. AWS, AGNTCY, Beeceptor, n8n, the evaluation runner.

Red flags to catch in review

  • A central decider sneaking in: anything that commits an organization without that organization's own approval.
  • An auto-commit, for example "if no answer by 5 pm, assign it to the nearest food bank."
  • An invented number: a quantity or time coming from model text instead of a calculator.
  • An approval not tied to a version.
  • Crop names in rules, such as "if crop == romaine." Use the profile instead.
  • Private data reaching an agent: anything beyond counts about households.
  • Silent waste: produce with no recorded pathway.
  • Promising produce that isn't committed yet.
