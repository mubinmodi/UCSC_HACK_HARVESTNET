Proving it works

The comparisons, the scenarios, the metrics, and what to do if negotiation loses.

After this lessonYou can explain what HarvestNet is compared against, what's measured, and why losing is an acceptable result.

Four methods, same inputs

  • First-come-first-served: the first food bank to claim gets the whole batch. This is the headline comparison.
  • Nearest food bank.
  • Rules-based negotiators: the same process and calculators, with no AI.
  • LLM negotiators: HarvestNet as designed.

Every method gets the same inputs at the same times, with the same rules and resources.

Five scenarios

A normal afternoon, more produce than storage, more households than produce, a cooler failure, and a condition doubt or no agreement.

What's measured

  • Produce wasted: pounds unclaimed or spoiled. Proposed as a headline result.
  • Worst-served fair-share coverage: how well the most-behind food bank's households did. Proposed as the second headline.
  • Households actually reached, counted by confirmed pickups.
  • Pickup-window match, time to commitment, and number-check failures.
  • Invalid commitments, which must be zero.

If negotiation loses

Say so. The judges score "learning and evidence," and an honest result beats a tuned one. A useful finding might be: "Rules-based negotiation matched the LLM on waste. The LLM's value was reading messy input and explaining decisions."

Cost

A batch costs an estimated $0.50–1.00 in Claude calls, so the $100 credit covers roughly 100–200 batches. Rules-based runs and replays cost nothing, so use those for most testing. These are estimates, not measurements.
