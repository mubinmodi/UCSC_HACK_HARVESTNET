The 30-second pitch

Four beats you can say from memory, and the claims to avoid.

After this lessonYou can pitch HarvestNet in about 30 seconds, in four beats, without overclaiming.

The four beats

  • Problem. Farms, grocers, and farmers markets end up with good produce they can't sell, and it spoils within days. Nearby, low-income families would use it. The hard part is matching the right produce to the right place before it spoils.
  • Who negotiates. Each donor and each food bank has its own AI agent. They negotiate how each batch is split, item by item, and who moves it.
  • Fairness. Each household has a weekly fair share that grows with household size, like a SNAP benefit. An advocate agent pushes produce toward the households furthest below it.
  • Why it's different. Nobody sits in the middle deciding. Every organization approves its own commitments. We measure it against first-come-first-served and report whatever we find.

Source: docs/MVP_DESIGN.md, section 1.

The script

"Every week, good fruit and vegetables rot while families nearby go without. The problem isn't supply. It's matching the right produce to the right food bank before it spoils. HarvestNet gives every donor and every food bank its own AI agent. They negotiate how each batch is split and who moves it, guided by a SNAP-style fair share for each household. There's no central decider: every organization keeps its own keys, and the food still moves."

What not to say

  • "The AI decides who gets food." It doesn't. Agents negotiate, and each organization's human approves.
  • "We're the first ever." No one has checked that. Say what's different instead.
  • Any result number before you've measured it. Cost and impact figures in the design are estimates or synthetic.
  • "We fed X people." We count households that confirmed a pickup, not meals eaten.

Why: the honesty rules in ADR 0005.
