The five workflows

Each workflow in plain steps, and who decides at each one.

After this lessonYou can walk through all five workflows and name who makes the decision in each.

1. A household asks for produce

The household calls or uses the web page. The assistant drafts its request, reads it back, asks for consent, and saves it under a short code, with no name. The household hears its fair share, chooses food banks, and then either gets a pickup now or is told it'll get a text.

Who decides: the household, about its own request. The assistant only reports produce that's already committed.

2. A donor reports surplus, and the agents split it

A donor, such as a farmers market at closing time, reports its items and whether it can drop them off. Food banks bid item by item. The advocate proposes changes to close the biggest fair-share gap. The donor and the food banks agree who moves what. Then each organization's person approves.

Who decides: the donor and each food bank, for themselves. The advocate only proposes.

3. Pickup and confirmation

The produce arrives. A calculator splits it into household portions, each up to that household's fair share. Households get a text with a code. When they show the code, their weekly balance updates. If a household says it can't make it, its portion goes to the next household furthest below fair share. SNAP households may get a Market Match tip.

Who decides: the calculator applies the published rule, and each household decides whether it's coming.

4. A food bank's cooler fails

A temperature reading (from Beeceptor in the demo) shows the cooler is too warm. The food bank's specialists check which items need cold. Romaine does, apples don't. The agent searches the Directory for food banks with cold space, writes a reason card for every candidate, and proposes a new version. Everyone involved approves again, and affected households get a text.

Who decides: the affected food bank proposes, and the receiving food bank decides whether to accept.

5. Condition doubt, or no agreement

When the produce's condition is uncertain or a record is missing, a person checks it. They either approve it as "use today" or decline it, with a reason. Recalled produce is never offered. If three rounds pass with no new terms, the result is a preserved disagreement.

Who decides: the donor's or food bank's person.
