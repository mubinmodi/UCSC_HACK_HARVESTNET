HarvestNet at a glance

Surplus fresh produce reaches low-income households at food banks they choose, instead of going to waste. Donor and food bank agents negotiate the split, guided by a SNAP-style fair share. There's no central decider.

One line: "Every organization keeps its own keys: nothing in HarvestNet can commit a donor or a food bank to a deal it didn't approve."

Agents

  • Donor: wants produce gone with little effort. Votes for itself.
  • Food bank: wants produce its households will use. Votes for itself.
  • Advocate: pushes toward the biggest fair-share gap. No vote.
  • Front-desk assistant: takes requests. Never allocates.

Humans who approve

  • Donor staff: promises and drop-offs.
  • Food bank managers: large deliveries, "use soon" produce, truck runs.

SNAP, three ways

  • Fair share: weekly pounds by household size. A published shared rule that assigns nothing.
  • Eligibility signal: optional, self-reported, never changes the share.
  • Produce programs: farmers markets as donors, plus Market Match tips.

Matching rules

  • Agents read produce profiles, never crop names.
  • Split each batch item by item, and agree who moves it (the donor drops off, or the food bank collects).
  • Only to households that can collect before the produce spoils. Fridge-only produce goes to limited-fridge households same-day only.
  • Every pound gets a recorded pathway. No silent waste.

When a deal is final

  • Every named organization has approved the same version, and every hold is valid.
  • Any change creates a new version, so approvals must be given again.
  • Three rounds with no new terms: preserved disagreement.
  • Deadline: unclaimed, with a reason. Never an auto-commit.

Levels

  • L1: specialists inside each agent.
  • L2: a cooler fails, then Directory rediscovery with reason cards.
  • L3: donors and food banks negotiate with no central decider, compared against first-come-first-served.

Review red flags

A central decider, an auto-commit, an invented number, an approval not tied to a version, a crop name in a rule, private household data reaching an agent, silent waste, or promising produce that isn't committed yet.
